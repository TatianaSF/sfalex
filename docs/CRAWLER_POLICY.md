# Crawler policy

The production `robots.txt` retains the existing `User-agent: *` and `Allow: /` policy and advertises the sitemap. That shared allow applies to compliant crawlers that use the wildcard group; this change adds no vendor-specific training directive or new robots rule.

Crawler products and user-fetch agents have different purposes and use distinct user-agent tokens. OpenAI distinguishes OAI-SearchBot for search, GPTBot for potential training use, and ChatGPT-User for user-initiated fetches. Anthropic distinguishes Claude-SearchBot, ClaudeBot, and Claude-User. Perplexity distinguishes PerplexityBot for search from Perplexity-User for user-directed fetches. No per-agent policy was added, so the existing wildcard rule remains authoritative for compliant crawlers. Robots rules are requests to crawlers, not access control. The `llms.txt` and Markdown summaries are convenience documents; they do not guarantee discovery, citation, or model behavior. Private material must never be placed in the public artifact.

References: [Google robots.txt guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/robots/intro), [OpenAI crawler documentation](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots), [Anthropic crawler guidance](https://support.anthropic.com/en/articles/8896518-does-anthropic-crawl-data-from-the-web-and-how-can-site-owners-block-the-crawler), and [Perplexity crawler documentation](https://docs.perplexity.ai/docs/resources/perplexity-crawlers).
